What is anxiety?

First things first: anxiety isn't a flaw, and it isn't dangerous. It's something every single person feels.

Anxiety is your body's built-in alarm system. When your brain thinks something might be a threat, it flips the alarm on to get you ready — heart faster, muscles tense, senses sharp. That's your body trying to protect you.

Here's the tricky part: the alarm can go off even when there's no real danger — like a smoke alarm that beeps just because you're making toast. The feeling is completely real, but the danger isn't always there.

Everyone feels anxious sometimes. It becomes something worth working on when it gets too big, happens too often, or starts stopping you from doing things you want to do. The good news: anxiety is very workable — and that's what this book is for.

In your body

Racing heart, tummy aches, tense shoulders, shaky hands, feeling hot or dizzy.

In your thoughts

"What if something goes wrong?" Worries that loop, expecting the worst, hard to focus.

In what you do

Avoiding, freezing up, needing lots of reassurance, or snapping and shutting down.

The anxiety iceberg

Anxiety doesn't always look like worry. On the outside, people might see anger, silliness, or someone who "won't even try." But that's just the tip. The real feelings are hidden below the surface.

Above the surface

What people see

  • Anger or meltdowns
  • Avoiding or refusing
  • Going quiet / shutting down
  • Silliness or distraction
  • "I don't care" · "I can't"

Below the surface

What's really going on

  • Feeling scared or unsafe
  • Worrying about failing
  • Feeling overwhelmed
  • Feeling not good enough
  • Needing help, unsure how to ask

For grown-ups: the behavior on top is usually the part that's hardest to be around — but it's rarely the real problem. When we respond to the feeling underneath instead of only the behavior above, kids feel understood, and the behavior tends to settle.
